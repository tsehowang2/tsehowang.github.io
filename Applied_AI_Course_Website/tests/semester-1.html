Semester 1 examination

Two hours · 100 points · Attempt without notes or an LLM.

Question 1 Language modeling and representations 15 points

Using “I go to school by bus,” explain next-token training and give shifted inputs/targets (5). Distinguish token IDs from embeddings; calculate cosine([3,4],[1,0]) and interpret it (3). For correct-token probabilities [.5,.25], calculate mean NLL and perplexity (2). For label order [bus, train, bicycle] and probabilities [.6,.2,.2], calculate negative log likelihood when train is the target and interpret lower loss (5).

Question 2 Attention by hand 20 points

A query produces unscaled scores [2,0] with d_head=4 and values [2,0] and [0,4]. Calculate scaled scores, stable softmax and the mixed output (10). Mask the second key as a future position and recalculate (6). Explain the different roles of Q, K and V even when they come from the same input (4).

Question 3 Transformer architecture 20 points

For B=2,T=128,d_model=256,H=8 give split Q/K/V, score, per-head output and merged output shapes (8). Compute explicitly stored float32 score memory in bytes and MiB (4). Explain residual, normalization and positionwise MLP paths in a pre-normalized block (4). Count attention-plus-MLP matrices for standard MHA d_model=64 and a two-matrix MLP of width256, excluding biases/norms; state one variant that changes this (4).

Question 4 Training a tiny transformer 20 points

Give training-loop pseudocode with shifted labels, causal masking, cross entropy, backward pass and optimizer update (8). Describe a future-token perturbation check and a one-batch overfit diagnostic (6). Explain grouped train/dev splitting and why lower training loss does not prove generalization (6).

Question 5 Generation and instruction following 15 points

Compare greedy and sampling with temperature (2). For [.5,.3,.15,.05], calculate top-k=2 and top-p=.7 distributions using the smallest sorted prefix reaching the threshold (3). Explain what a KV cache stores and why it speeds decoding without learning new facts (4). Distinguish pretraining from instruction SFT (3). Explain why fluent chain-of-thought text does not establish correctness (3).

Question 6 Beyond text and experimental reasoning 10 points

Compare autoregressive image-token generation with diffusion (3). Explain recurrent state, vanishing gradients, and the purpose of LSTM/GRU gates (3). Contrast encoder-only, decoder-only and encoder-decoder visibility (4).

Q1 (15). Inputs I/go/to/school/by, targets go/to/school/by/bus. Each prefix predicts the following token (5). IDs index learned rows (1). Norm([3,4])=5, cosine=3/5=.6, a direction comparison (2). Mean NLL=(ln2+ln4)/2≈1.03972, perplexity=exp(1.03972)≈2.82843 (2). -ln(.2)=1.6094 nats. Lower loss means more probability on the observed target, not proof of factual truth (5).

Q2 (20). Divide by sqrt(4)=2: [1,0]. Stable exponents [1,.367879], normalized [.731059,.268941]. Output [1.462118,1.075764]. Scaling 2, weights 4, output 4 (10 total). Masked scores [1,-infinity], probabilities [1,0], output [2,0] (6). Q asks what is needed, K is scored for compatibility, V supplies the mixed payload; distinct projections enable the roles (4).

Q3 (20). Q/K/V [2,8,128,32], scores [2,8,128,128], head output [2,8,128,32], merged [2,128,256] (8). 262144 elements times four =1048576 bytes=1 MiB (4). x becomes x+Attention(LN(x)), then x+MLP(LN(x)); MLP acts per position and residual preserves a direct path (4). Attention matrices=4×64²=16,384; MLP=2×64×256=32,768; total49,152. GQA changes K/V count or a gated MLP adds a matrix (4).

Q4 (20). Shift batches; forward with causal mask; flatten position/vocabulary logits against aligned target IDs; cross entropy; zero gradients; backward; optional clipping; optimizer step (8). Future perturbation must preserve earlier logits with fixed parameters/eval mode; a tiny one-batch dataset should be overfittable as a wiring diagnostic (6). Split source groups before making overlapping windows. Memorization and leakage can lower training loss without useful generalization (6).

Q5 (15). Greedy selects the maximum; sampling draws from probabilities; lower temperature sharpens (2). Top-k=2 retains .5,.3, divides by .8 and gives [.625,.375,0,0]. Top-p=.7 reaches the threshold with the same two entries and gives the same distribution (3). The cache stores prior keys/values, avoiding recomputation; parameters remain fixed (4). Pretraining learns continuation; instruction SFT updates parameters from desired responses, commonly applying loss to response tokens (3). A rationale is generated text; verify answers independently with controlled comparisons (3).

Q6 (10). Autoregression predicts image tokens; diffusion learns a reverse/noise-prediction process (3). Recurrence updates a compressed state; repeated Jacobians can shrink gradients; LSTM/GRU gates regulate information flow (3). Encoder self-attention sees the whole input; decoder self-attention sees the prefix; encoder-decoder adds target queries over source K/V (4).
