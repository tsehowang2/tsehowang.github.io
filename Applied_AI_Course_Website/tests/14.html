Question 1

Distinguish pretraining, SFT, in-context examples, and preference learning.

Show worked solution

Pretraining learns broad prediction patterns; SFT optimizes desired target responses; prompt demonstrations affect current context without gradient updates; preference methods learn from judged alternatives.

Question 2

A ticket costs 4 dollars and three tickets are required. Give the answer and design a wrong fluent rationale.

Show worked solution

Correct=12 dollars. Any plausible-looking explanation ending with an incorrect amount illustrates why fluency is not a correctness checker.

Question 3

Why are displayed reasoning steps neither proof of correctness nor a faithful guaranteed account of internal causality?

Show worked solution

They are generated outputs that can be wrong or rationalized. Compare independent final checks and full inference costs; reasoning training, prompts, and verification are distinct interventions.
